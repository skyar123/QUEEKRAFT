## The Ballroom Scene & House Mothers

### Crystal LaBeija (1970s – present)
**House Mother, Founder of the House of LaBeija**

Crystal LaBeija is widely credited with founding the Ballroom scene in New York City in the early 1970s after she and other Black and Latinx queens were excluded from mainstream drag pageants. Furious at the racism of the predominantly white circuit, Crystal created an alternative — a space where BIPOC queer and trans people could compete, perform, and crown each other.

The **House system** she pioneered became a cornerstone of trans survival: houses function as chosen-family units, each led by a Mother (and sometimes a Father) who mentors, protects, and advocates for their "children." House Mothers provide housing, food, safety, and emotional support to trans youth who have been rejected by their biological families.

Crystal's legacy lives in every House — LaBeija, Xtravaganza, Ninja, Ebony, Corey, Pendavis — and in the culture of *reading*, *vogueing*, and *ballroom balls* that continues today through shows like *Pose* and *Legendary*.

**In-game item:** LaBeija's Trophy (Legendary loot — AOE stun + full heal)

### Angie Xtravaganza (c. 1965–1993)
**House Mother of the House of Xtravaganza**

Angie Xtravaganza was one of the most beloved figures in Ballroom culture, founding the House of Xtravaganza and raising generations of trans women of color through the height of the AIDS crisis. She was featured prominently in Jennie Livingston's 1990 documentary *Paris Is Burning*, which brought Ballroom culture to international attention.

As a House Mother, Angie provided her children not just with a House name but with family — teaching survival, self-presentation, and community care during one of the most devastating periods in queer history. She died of AIDS-related complications in 1993. Her House continues under her children's stewardship.

Her story is a study in **motherhood as political act**: in a world that was letting her community die, she chose to nurture.

**Game NPC key:** `angie_xtravaganza`  
**In-game item:** House Mother's Sash (Epic loot — defense buff)

### MJ Rodriguez (born 1990)
**Actress — Blanca Evangelista in *Pose***

MJ Rodriguez is an American actress and singer who came to prominence playing Blanca Evangelista, a House Mother in the FX series *Pose* (2018–2021). In 2022, she became the **first transgender woman to win a Golden Globe Award** for Best Actress in a Drama Series.

*Pose* was a landmark production set in 1980s–90s New York Ballroom culture, featuring the largest cast of transgender actors in a television series. Blanca Evangelista — whom Rodriguez played — creates her own House specifically to mother trans youth dying of AIDS, choosing **love and chosen family over survival odds**.

Rodriguez's performance brought Ballroom motherhood to a global mainstream audience, honoring the real House Mothers while giving trans youth today a visible image of themselves as worthy of love and legacy.

**Game NPC key:** `mj_rodriguez`  
**In-game item:** LaBeija's Trophy (shared legacy)

## STAR House & Activist Mothers

### Marsha P. Johnson (1945–1992)
**"True Drag Mother," STAR Co-Founder**
*(Already in game — expanded lore)*

Marsha P. Johnson's iconic "P stands for Pay It No Mind" was more than a catchphrase — it was a survival philosophy. But alongside her public persona as a flamboyant street queen at Stonewall, Marsha was a ferocious **caretaker of homeless trans youth**.

STAR — **Street Transvestite Action Revolutionaries** — which she co-founded with Sylvia Rivera in 1970, was born from a direct maternal impulse: she and Sylvia had been sleeping on the streets as teenagers and refused to let the next generation do the same. They organized, squatted a building in Greenwich Village, turned it into a shelter, and **mothered dozens of homeless young trans people** through one of the most hostile eras in American history.

Marsha's motherhood was radical, improvisational, and absolute. She gave everything she had, repeatedly. Her murder in 1992 — ruled a suicide, later reclassified — remains unsolved.

---

### Sylvia Rivera (1951–2002)
**STAR Co-Founder, Maternal Activist**
*(Already in game — expanded lore)*

Sylvia Rivera described herself as a "street gay," a "drag queen," and a mother. At 11, she was homeless in New York City. At 17, she was at Stonewall. At 19, she co-founded STAR with Marsha.

The STAR House embodied Sylvia's belief that **trans liberation must start with the most marginalized**: homeless youth, sex workers, people of color. It was not a charitable organization run by others — it was their own, by and for the community. Sylvia and Marsha shoplifted, hustled, and begged to keep the rent paid.

After years of marginalization even within the gay rights movement — which repeatedly tried to push trans people to the back — Sylvia spent her final years fighting for inclusion and running STAR as a community organization.

### Kenya Cuevas (born c. 1980s)
**Founder of Casa de las Muñecas Tiresias, Mexico**

Kenya Cuevas is a Mexican trans activist whose path to advocacy began with grief. When her best friend, a trans woman known as La Parado, was murdered in 2016, Kenya vowed to transform that loss into protection for others.

She founded **Casa de las Muñecas Tiresias** (House of the Tiresias Dolls) — a shelter specifically for trans women in Mexico — and **Mexico's first trans mausoleum**, a space to honor the trans women killed by violence. In a country where trans women face extreme rates of murder, Kenya's work is both memorial and survival infrastructure.

She organized Mexico City's first trans-specific march and has become one of Latin America's most visible trans rights advocates. Her work embodies the principle: **no one dies alone while I'm standing**.

**In-game item:** The Mausoleum Flower (Legendary — regen + memorial particles)

## Trans Mothers Worldwide

### Gauri Sawant (born 1980)
**Indian Trans Mother — Adopted Daughter Gayatri**

Gauri Sawant is a Mumbai-based transgender activist who became a mother when Gayatri, a young girl whose mother had died, asked Gauri to become her mother. Gauri said yes.

The relationship between Gauri and Gayatri was not legally recognized for years — India's courts and social systems actively resisted it. But Gauri fought, petitioned, and refused to give up. She submitted a petition to the Supreme Court of India as part of the **National Legal Services Authority (NALSA) case**, which resulted in a landmark 2014 ruling recognizing a "third gender" and affirming trans rights.

A **Vicks "Touch of Care"** advertising campaign featuring Gauri and Gayatri went viral internationally, reaching millions of viewers and becoming a cultural touchstone for trans motherhood. The message was simple and revolutionary: **a mother's love has no gender**.

**Game NPC key:** `gauri_sawant`  
**In-game item:** Vicks Touch of Care (Uncommon — +2 heal + regen)

### Mariela Muñoz (c. 1940s–2020s)
**Argentine Trans Mother — Raised 23 Children**

Mariela Muñoz was an Argentine transgender woman who, over the course of her lifetime, became the mother of **twenty-three children** — all of them abandoned by their biological families or left without support by the system. She took them in, one by one, giving each of them food, shelter, love, and a place to belong.

Argentina has one of Latin America's more progressive legal frameworks for trans rights (the **Gender Identity Law of 2012** allows self-identification without surgery or psychiatric diagnosis). Mariela's life exemplifies what was possible when trans people chose community over survival alone. Her motherhood was not exceptional to her — it was simply what she did because children needed a home and she had one.

She is a testament to **chosen family at scale**: not a program, not an organization, just a trans woman who refused to turn away a child in need.

**Game NPC key:** `mariela_munoz`  
**In-game item:** Mariela's Tarot Deck (Rare — fortune item with random effect)

### Gloria "Mama Gloria" Allen (born 1946)
**Founder of Chicago Trans Youth Charm School**

Gloria Allen — known throughout Chicago's trans community as **Mama Gloria** — began teaching a charm school for transgender youth in the 1960s and continued for nearly forty years. Her curriculum was etiquette, self-presentation, and grace; her real subject was survival.

"When you carry yourself with grace, the world has a harder time denying you exist," she has said. In an era when trans people were criminalized simply for existing, Mama Gloria taught her students to hold their heads up, to make eye contact, to move through the world with unapologetic dignity. This was not accommodation — it was **armor**.

Her story inspired the award-winning play *The Charm* and has been celebrated in oral histories, memorials, and by every trans woman in Chicago who called her by name.

**Game NPC key:** `mama_gloria`  
**In-game item:** Mama Gloria's Charm Book (Rare — heal + permanent HP)

### Cleopatra Kambugu (born c. 1985)
**Ugandan Trans Rights Pioneer**

Cleopatra Kambugu is a Ugandan transgender woman and rights advocate, subject of the internationally acclaimed documentary *The Pearl of Africa* (2016), which followed her life in Kampala. Uganda has some of the world's most hostile legislation toward LGBTQ+ people; simply being out as trans is an act of profound courage.

Kambugu has become one of East Africa's most visible trans advocates, working at the intersection of trans rights and HIV/AIDS activism. Her visibility — chosen deliberately despite the risks — operates on the principle that **every trans person who lives openly makes it slightly safer for the next**.

**Game NPC key:** `cleopatra_kambugu`

## Historical Trans Figures

### We'wha (1849–1896)
**Zuni Lhamana, Cultural Ambassador**

We'wha was a Zuni lhamana — a person who combined aspects of both male and female Zuni gender roles, living fluidly across what Western culture would call a gender binary. This was not transgression within Zuni society but **sacred recognition**: some people are called to carry both, and this is understood as spiritual significance.

We'wha was a master weaver and potter, and became a **cultural ambassador** who traveled to Washington D.C. in 1886 and spent six months meeting American politicians, intellectuals, and President Grover Cleveland. At a time when Indigenous peoples were being systematically dispossessed, We'wha moved through the halls of American power as a dignified representative of Zuni culture — and as exactly who they were.

We'wha's life is evidence that **gender fluidity is not a modern invention but an ancient human reality** — erased by colonialism, not created by modernity.

### Coccinelle (1931–2006)
**1950s French Trans Pioneer**

Jacqueline Charlotte Dufresnoy — known professionally as **Coccinelle** (the Ladybug) — was a French actress, cabaret performer, and trans pioneer who became an international celebrity in the 1950s and 1960s. She performed at the Carrousel de Paris, one of the few venues in Europe where trans women could be publicly celebrated at the time.

Coccinelle was among the first trans women in France to undergo gender-affirming surgery, in 1958 — surgery she sought out and obtained at enormous personal risk and cost in a country with almost no legal or medical framework for it. She married twice, lived openly as a woman throughout her career, and used her celebrity to speak about trans lives when almost no one else was.

### Jennifer Finney Boylan (born 1958)
**Author, Activist, First Trans Bestselling Memoirist**

Jennifer Finney Boylan is an American author and professor whose 2003 memoir *She's Not There: A Life in Two Genders* was the **first bestselling book by a transgender American**. The book told the story of her transition with her wife and children, and its success brought trans experience into mainstream literary culture at a moment when trans stories were almost never told — certainly not warmly, certainly not from the inside.

She has served on the board of GLAAD, written for *The New York Times*, and spent decades using storytelling as a mode of trans advocacy. Her core message is that **love survives transformation** — that families can hold each other through change, and that trans people deserve to be seen fully and with complexity.

## Key Locations (In-Game Rooms)

### The Ballroom
A safe gathering space originating in 1970s New York, built by and for BIPOC queer and trans people. In the game, Ballroom rooms appear as gold-tinted, sparkle-lit spaces where enemies cannot enter. Ballroom NPCs (Crystal LaBeija, Angie Xtravaganza, MJ Rodriguez) may spawn here.

### STAR House
The shelter Marsha P. Johnson and Sylvia Rivera built in Greenwich Village — a squat, a home, a survival infrastructure. In the game, STAR House rooms provide shelter and resources. Kenya Cuevas, Cleopatra Kambugu, and Mariela Muñoz may appear here.

### Mama Gloria's Charm School
A training space in Chicago's South Side. In the game, Charm School rooms are warm-teal spaces where Mama Gloria teaches. The Charm Book drops here.

### Casa de las Muñecas Tiresias
Kenya Cuevas's shelter for trans women in Mexico City. Referenced in game murals and in Kenya's dialogue.

### Aaji Cha Ghar (Grandma's Home)
Gauri Sawant's foster home in India. Referenced in game murals and in Gauri's dialogue.
